Workbook  ·  Introduction

The playbook describes the five BE behaviors and how students grow in them over time. This workbook is where you put them to work in one of your own courses. Each exercise ends with something you can use right away: a syllabus statement, a revised assignment, or a plan for the term.

You don't need to be an AI expert to use it, and you don't need to use AI yourself. The workbook is about how your students make decisions about AI, and how your course can help them make good ones.

Why "BE the Change"

The name comes from OCTC's faculty workshop on AI literacy. The workshop moves through three roles. In BE the Student, faculty use AI as learners, on a task outside their expertise. In BE the Teacher, they look back at that experience and ask what it tells them about their students. In BE the Change, they commit to one change in a course they teach.

This workbook is the third part. It's where that one change gets built.

If you attended the workshop

Get out your One-Change Card. Your behavior, course, and date are already decided. Go to Exercise 2 and build the change you wrote down.

If you didn't

You haven't missed anything you need. Everything here works on its own. Pick one assignment you already teach and start with the path below.

One Change, Not a List

There are four exercises, but you don't need to do all four. The workbook is built around one change in one course. Exercise 2 is where that change happens. The others help you set it up, see whether it's working, and talk with students about it.

  1. Exercise 1

    Write Your AI Use Statement

    Start here if your syllabus doesn't yet say how AI fits your course.

    You'll leave with a syllabus statement, a sentence students can use to tell you how they used AI, and a label for each main assignment.

  2. Exercise 2 · The core

    One Meaningful Change

    Start here if you already have an AI policy, or you're coming from the workshop with a card.

    You'll leave with one assignment revised with TILT around one BE behavior, ready to paste into Blackboard, and a date to have it live.

  3. Exercise 3

    Build Your Checkpoints

    Come here when your change is in place and you want to see how students' judgment develops across the term.

    You'll leave with a plan of four or five checkpoints tied to assignments you already give.

  4. Exercise 4 · Coming soon

    Talk with Students About AI

    Come here when you want to be ready for conversations about AI, from everyday questions to a concern about an assignment.

    You'll leave with a plan for how you'll open those conversations and where your role ends and the integrity process begins.

How the Workbook Works
  • Each exercise has three parts: a short lesson, a set of steps to work through, and a Copilot prompt if you'd rather talk it through.
  • Your answers stay on your computer. They save automatically in the browser you're using, and nothing is sent anywhere. That also means they won't follow you to another computer or browser, and clearing your browser data erases them.
  • To keep a copy, use Print or save as PDF on any exercise. The printout includes only your answers.
  • With the Copilot prompts, don't paste in student names or student work, and read every draft before you use it.
  • Keep the playbook open alongside. The five BE pages describe each behavior at each stage and are the best place to look when you're choosing a behavior.
