Appendix B
The BE Framework at OCTC
At OCTC, the BE Framework is the student part of a college-wide AI literacy framework that also covers faculty and staff. All three share the same five areas of AI judgment and the same three-level scale. This appendix shows how the playbook lines up with that framework.
Faculty Guide  ·  OCTC  ·  Draft
Draft. This appendix reflects the OCTC AI Literacy Framework review draft from September 2026. It will be updated when the framework is final. Other colleges adapting this playbook can replace or remove this page.
How the BE Behaviors Connect

Each BE behavior is the student version of one of the college’s five shared dimensions. Faculty and staff have their own versions of the same five.

BE behaviors mapped to OCTC shared dimensions
BE behaviorOCTC shared dimensionWhat it covers
BE Safe Understanding & Safety Knowing enough about how AI works, including what it does with your information, to decide whether and how to use it.
BE Honest Integrity & Transparency Using AI honestly and being accurate about what you did and what AI did.
BE Critical Critical Judgment Checking AI output against what you know before acting on it.
BE Responsible Intentional Use & Responsibility Choosing on purpose when AI fits the work, and owning the result.
BE Reflective Reflection & Growth Noticing how using AI, or not using it, is affecting your learning and your work, and adjusting.
Stages and Levels

The playbook describes three stages students move through as they build each habit. The college framework uses a three-level scale for self-assessment. The two line up like this:

Playbook stages mapped to OCTC framework levels
Playbook stageOCTC levelWhat the level means
Introduction Level 1: Beginning Starting to notice and use the behavior, sometimes on their own and often with a nudge.
Exploration (early) Level 1: Beginning Practicing across courses, still inconsistently.
Exploration (by the end) Level 2: Practicing Using the behavior in most situations with reasonable consistency. This is OCTC’s proposed target.
Application Level 3: Applying Using the behavior independently and consistently, with clear reasoning, including in new situations and in their field.

Both describe the quality of a student’s judgment, not how often they use AI.

Students Who Don’t Use AI

Not using AI is recorded as a status, not a level, because how often someone uses AI and how well they judge it are two different things.

Not yet considered. The student doesn’t use AI and hasn’t had a reason yet to think about whether it belongs in their work. That’s not a shortcoming. Usually it just means no one has asked. A student who uses AI regularly, even without thinking much about it, is on the scale rather than in this status.

Considered and declined. The student has thought it through and decided not to use AI for a particular course, task, or stretch of time. That can be a sign of strong judgment, so this status can go with any level on the scale.

For Development, Not Grading

The framework’s levels are for self-assessment and growth. No BE behavior is graded unless a course writes it into a student learning outcome. When that happens, it’s assessed as that outcome, the same way as any other.

This guide supports the BE Framework, a student-centered translation of the Digital Education Council (DEC) AI Literacy Framework.
